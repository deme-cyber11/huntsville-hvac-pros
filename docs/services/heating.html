Heating Repair & Installation in Huntsville, AL — Ready Before the Cold Hits

Huntsville's winters are shorter than its summers, but they're cold enough. When a furnace or heat pump fails during a January cold snap, a house loses heat fast. Heating repair covers the diagnosis and correction of whatever failed — igniter, heat exchanger, blower motor, gas valve, or refrigerant issue for heat pumps. Most heating failures are diagnosable and repairable in one visit if parts are available.

When You Need Heating Service

  • System runs but blows cold or room-temperature air — burner not lighting, limit switch tripped, or heat pump in cooling mode
  • Unit cycles on and off without reaching the set temperature — often a dirty filter blocking airflow or a failing heat exchanger
  • Burning electrical smell during operation — stop the system and call; this is not normal
  • Yellow or flickering burner flame — gas furnace flames should be steady blue; yellow indicates incomplete combustion
  • System won't start at all when outdoor temps drop — thermostat, igniter, or control board issue
  • Electric bill spikes without increased thermostat use — a heat pump running in emergency heat mode constantly or a system working harder than it should
Gas smell near your furnace? Leave the house immediately. Don't flip any switches. Call your gas company from outside, then call us. Do not re-enter until the gas company clears the building.

How Heating Repair Works

  1. Diagnostic: We check the igniter, heat exchanger, blower motor, gas valve, and limit switches on furnaces. For heat pumps: refrigerant level, reversing valve, defrost board, and compressor health.
  2. Written quote: Exact repair cost in writing before any work starts. You approve the number; work begins only after your sign-off.
  3. Repair: Common parts — igniters, blower motors, control boards, contactors — are often stocked on the service van for same-day completion.
  4. Safety test: For gas furnaces, we run a combustion analysis and CO check before clearing the system as operational.

What Every Heating Service Includes

  • Full system diagnostic — gas or heat pump
  • CO and combustion safety check for gas systems
  • Written repair quote before work begins
  • Parts and labor for the approved repair
  • Thermostat function test
  • Post-repair performance verification — system reaches set temperature before we leave
  • Written documentation of findings and work completed

What Does Heating Service Cost?

Most furnace and heat pump repairs in Huntsville run $150–$500, depending on what failed. Igniter replacement typically runs $150–$250. Blower motor replacement runs $250–$450. Gas valve replacement runs $350–$500. Full furnace replacement runs $2,500–$5,000 installed. A full HVAC system — heating and cooling — runs $7,000–$14,000 installed. We quote in writing; the price you approve is the price you pay.

Common Questions About Heating Service

Why is my furnace blowing cold air?

Most common causes: a failed igniter (the furnace tries to start but can't light the burner), a tripped high-limit switch caused by a dirty filter restricting airflow, or a thermostat set to "fan" instead of "heat." All are diagnosable and typically repairable same-day.

How long does heating repair take?

Most repairs — igniter, limit switch, blower motor, control board — take 1–3 hours. If a specific part needs to be ordered, a follow-up visit is required. We'll let you know upfront before you commit to the service call.

Is a heat pump or a gas furnace better for Huntsville winters?

Both work well in North Alabama. Heat pumps are more efficient down to about 35°F, below which a gas backup or electric auxiliary heat takes over. Gas furnaces provide stronger output on the coldest nights but cost more per BTU to run at mild temperatures. When replacement time comes, we'll help you compare options honestly.

My heat pump is running constantly but barely heating — what's wrong?

When outdoor temps drop below 35–40°F, heat pumps work harder and may struggle. If it's mild outside and the system still can't keep up, suspect low refrigerant, a failing reversing valve, or a defrost cycle that's stuck or not working. Call — this is diagnosable in one visit.
