Furnace Repair

Gas and electric furnaces are the most common heating systems in Huntsville homes. When yours fails, these are the issues we see most often:

Ignition failures

the furnace tries to start but never lights. Usually a faulty igniter, flame sensor, or gas valve issue.

Blower motor problems

the furnace lights but does not push warm air through the ducts. A failing blower motor or capacitor is typically the cause.

Short cycling

the furnace starts, runs briefly, then shuts off. Often caused by a dirty flame sensor, clogged filter, or overheating issue.

Cracked heat exchanger

a serious safety concern. A cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide into your home. We check this on every furnace call.

Thermostat issues

sometimes the furnace is fine and the thermostat is the problem. We check this first to save you an unnecessary repair bill.

Heat Pump Service

Heat pumps are increasingly popular in North Alabama because they handle both heating and cooling in a single system. In Huntsville's mild winters, a heat pump is often the most energy-efficient choice for year-round comfort.

Not heating or cooling properly

low refrigerant, a failing reversing valve, or compressor issues

Frozen outdoor unit

defrost cycle failure, restricted airflow, or low charge

Auxiliary heat running constantly

the system is relying on backup electric strips instead of the heat pump. This spikes your electric bill and indicates the heat pump itself needs attention.

Strange noises during operation

a failing compressor, loose fan blade, or worn bearings

Heating Service FAQ

Answers to the questions Huntsville homeowners ask most about heating systems.

Annually. The best time to service your furnace is in the fall, before you need it. An annual tune-up catches small problems before they become expensive repairs, verifies safe operation (including carbon monoxide testing), and keeps the system running efficiently through the winter months.
Heat pumps are more efficient in mild climates like Huntsville because they move heat rather than generate it. For most of our winter, a heat pump handles heating efficiently. On the coldest nights (below 30 degrees), a dual-fuel system -- heat pump plus gas furnace backup -- gives you the best of both worlds: efficiency most of the time, and reliable gas heat when temperatures drop sharply.
Watch for these warning signs: strange noises when the system starts or runs (banging, squealing, rattling), uneven heating between rooms, a noticeable increase in your energy bills without changes in usage, the system cycling on and off frequently, yellow or flickering pilot light on gas furnaces (should be blue), and any smell of gas or burning near the unit. If you smell gas, leave the house and call both your gas company and us immediately.
