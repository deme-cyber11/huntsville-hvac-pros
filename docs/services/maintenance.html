HVAC Maintenance Plans in Huntsville, AL — Catch Problems Before They Catch You

North Alabama's cooling season runs April through October — six to seven months of daily operation in 85–95°F heat and high humidity. A system that isn't tuned annually works harder than it needs to, wears components faster, and loses efficiency month over month. HVAC maintenance catches small problems — dirty coils, low refrigerant, weakening capacitors — before they become mid-July emergencies.

Signs Your System Is Overdue for Service

  • You can't remember the last time a technician looked at it
  • Your electric bill is higher this summer than last, with no change in how you use the system
  • The system runs longer than it used to to reach the same temperature
  • You've had one or more repair calls in the past two years
  • The outdoor unit is visibly dirty or has debris around the base
  • The system is 5+ years old with no documented service history
  • Your air filter looks like it hasn't been changed in months

Most components that cause breakdowns fail gradually, not suddenly. A capacitor running at 60% capacity shows no symptoms you'd notice — until it fails on a 95°F Saturday in August. Maintenance catches these before they fail.

What a Maintenance Visit Covers

  1. System inspection: We check refrigerant levels, electrical connections, capacitor readings, contactor condition, coils (indoor and outdoor), condensate drain, and blower components.
  2. Cleaning: Outdoor coil cleaning, condensate drain flush, and drain pan inspection. Blower wheel cleaning if buildup is present.
  3. Calibration: Thermostat calibration, airflow verification, and refrigerant pressure confirmation to manufacturer spec.
  4. Written condition report: Every visit produces a written report with findings. If something is marginal, we note it with a repair estimate and honest guidance on urgency — no pressure.

What Every Maintenance Visit Includes

  • Refrigerant level check and pressure test
  • Electrical component inspection — capacitor, contactor, and wiring
  • Outdoor coil cleaning and condenser inspection
  • Condensate drain flush and pan inspection
  • Blower motor and belt inspection
  • Thermostat calibration and function test
  • Filter check and replacement recommendation
  • Written condition report with any findings or concerns

What Does HVAC Maintenance Cost?

A single maintenance visit (one system, one visit) runs $89–$149. Annual maintenance plans covering two visits per year — spring cooling tune-up and fall heating check — run $149–$249 per year and include priority scheduling and a discount on any repairs flagged during the visit. We don't upsell repairs you don't need; the written condition report documents exactly what we found.

Common Questions About HVAC Maintenance

How often should I have my HVAC system serviced?

Twice a year is the standard recommendation: once before cooling season (March–April) and once before heating season (October–November). If your system is over 10 years old, that schedule becomes even more critical — aging components need more frequent monitoring.

What's the actual benefit if my system seems to be running fine?

Most components that cause breakdowns fail gradually. A capacitor at 60% capacity doesn't cause symptoms you'd notice — until it fails on a summer Saturday. Annual maintenance identifies marginal components while you still have options and time. "Running fine" doesn't mean "running efficiently."

Do I need to be home for a maintenance visit?

Yes — someone needs to provide access to both the indoor air handler and outdoor condenser unit, and be available to review the written condition report at the end of the visit.

Will you try to sell me repairs I don't need?

No. The written condition report documents what we found. If something is marginal, we'll note it and tell you honestly whether it warrants immediate attention or monitoring. The repair decision is always yours. We don't work on commission.
